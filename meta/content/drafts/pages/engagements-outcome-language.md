# Content: Engagements rung copy — outcome-language pass (`/engagements`)

**Route**: `/engagements` (revision overlay on the four rung `delivers` / `deRisks` strings)
**Status**: Draft — Arian word-level approval required.
**Owner**: Arian (founder) · Author: pouk-ai-content
**Last updated**: 2026-06-15 (v0.1)
**Basis draft**: `meta/content/drafts/pages/engagements.md` (Approved v0.3 — the rung copy this overlay revises)
**Governing spec**: `meta/specs/pages/engagements-amendment-raise-the-ceiling.md` §4.3 + `meta/specs/features/outcome-language-pass.md`
**Content-data spec**: `meta/specs/content/engagements.json.md` (**schema unchanged** — `delivers`/`deRisks` string content only)

The raise-the-ceiling amendment (§4.3) asks the four rungs to read in **felt-outcome** terms — what the buyer *gets* and *stops worrying about* — rather than purely process terms. The current `delivers`/`deRisks` (Approved v0.3) are accurate but lean process-descriptive. This overlay lifts desire by naming the reader's relieved situation, **categorically, with zero figures** — and on `/engagements` the no-numerals guardrail is **absolute** (§7(a)). The single line not to cross: *the instant a felt-outcome line invents a number ("saves 10 hours a week"), the categorical-only contract is broken and the pass has failed.* The four rungs stay mutually distinguishable on the delivered-outcome / de-risked dimension.

Everything else in the Approved v0.3 base draft — hero, eyebrows (`The entry point` / `The proof` / `The build` / `The partnership`), CTA labels, end CTA, ladder index, SEO — is **unchanged and authoritative in that file**. This overlay touches only `delivers` and `deRisks`.

## 1. Drafting notes

- **Audience read**: the same Evaluation-stage operator the base draft addresses — they've matched themselves to an archetype and want the *shape* of the relationship legible. The shift: they should leave each rung wanting the *outcome* it produces, not just understanding the *process* (the amendment's "the work is felt, not just described").
- **Outcome read** (from amendment §4.3 + `outcome-language-pass.md` §4):
  - Each rung's `delivers`/`deRisks` reads in felt-outcome terms — what the buyer gets / stops worrying about.
  - **No invented metrics — hard guardrail.** No figure, percentage, time-saving, cost, or fabricated specificity in any rung copy. Categorical only (§7(a), absolute).
  - Copy-direction revision **within the existing schema** — no new field, no structural change.
  - The four rungs stay mutually distinguishable (engagements.md §5).
- **Voice anchor**: agent §4.6 (concrete over abstract — felt outcome named in operator terms, e.g. "the workflow that was eating a day a week, running itself" → reframed categorically per the spec's "drop the quantity" default), §4.4 (no marketing-speak — "running itself" is operator-felt; "transform your business" is the banned over-correction), §4.2 (operator-first). Mirrors the `/roles` outcome-language pass register and the existing `delivers`/`deRisks` voice.
- **Assumptions** (flag for Arian):
  - **A1 — the felt-outcome shift leads each `delivers` with the reader's relieved situation**, then keeps the process detail that makes the rung concrete and distinct (the amendment is a register shift, not a content cut — the rungs must stay distinguishable, so the mechanism stays visible).
  - **A2 — `deRisks` stays one sentence opening with a verb of removal** (the base draft's parallel-set discipline: "Lets you… / Proves… / Closes… / Keeps…"), but reframed toward the *felt* removed fear rather than the process description.
  - **A3 — the spec's "a day a week" illustration is NOT literalized into copy.** Per `outcome-language-pass.md` §4, "when in doubt, drop the quantity"; I name the *kind* of work ("the workflow that's been quietly eating the team's time") rather than any duration. Zero numerals.
  - **A4 — Build and Retainer keep their best existing phrases** ("a system that works in production, not a deck that describes one"; "quietly rotting") — these are already felt-outcome and already Arian-approved; the pass sharpens the *opening* toward the reader's situation without discarding what works.

## 2. Copy

Each rung labelled by `id`. **Before** = the Approved v0.3 string (the diff baseline); **After** = the felt-outcome revision (the shipping copy on approval). `id`, `eyebrow`, `title`, `cta` are UNCHANGED from v0.3.

### Rung: `discovery`

- **delivers — Before**: `A short, focused diagnosis of where your AI work actually stands — which of the five failure modes you're in, and a written read of the workflow, the data behind it, and what a fix would take. You leave with the diagnosis whether or not we build anything together.`
- **delivers — After (recommended)**: `Clarity about where your AI work actually stands — which of the five failure modes you're in, what's really blocking the workflow, and what a fix would take — written down, in your hands. You stop guessing and start with a map. You leave with that diagnosis whether or not we build anything together.`
- **deRisks — Before**: `Lets you understand the problem before committing to a build, so the first thing you buy is clarity, not a bet.`
- **deRisks — After (recommended, kept close — already felt)**: `Lets you buy clarity before you buy a build — so the first commitment is understanding the problem, not betting on a fix.`

### Rung: `pilot`

- **delivers — Before**: `A working result on one scoped workflow — built, measured against a baseline you set up front, and put in front of the team that would actually use it. One team, one process, real signal.`
- **delivers — After (recommended)**: `The one workflow you picked, working for real — built, put in front of the team that lives with it, and measured against the baseline you set up front. You get to see the approach prove itself on something that matters before you bet the rollout on it. One team, one process, real signal.`
- **deRisks — Before**: `Proves the approach on a single workflow before you roll it out anywhere else, so a full rollout rides on evidence instead of a pitch.`
- **deRisks — After (recommended, kept close — already felt)**: `Lets you watch the approach work on one real workflow before you stake a rollout on it — so the big commitment rides on evidence, not a pitch.`

### Rung: `build`

- **delivers — Before**: `The real system, engineered and shipped into production — wired into your stack, documented, and handed over so an in-house team can run it. A system that works in production, not a deck that describes one.`
- **delivers — After (recommended)**: `The real thing, running in production and wired into the tools you already use — not a demo, not a deck that describes one. It's engineered, documented, and handed over so your own team can run it without staying tethered to pouk.ai. The work finally lands where it earns its keep: in production.`
- **deRisks — Before**: `Closes the gap between "we launched an AI pilot" and "AI is delivering measurable value," so the work lands in production instead of stalling at the demo.`
- **deRisks — After (recommended, kept close — already the deployment-gap line)**: `Closes the gap between "we launched a pilot" and "it's actually delivering" — so the work ships and lands, instead of stalling at the demo like most pilots do.`

### Rung: `retainer`

- **delivers — Before**: `Ongoing operation and iteration of what's shipped — monitoring, tuning, and extending the system as the business changes and the tools move underneath it. The relationship that keeps the work paying off after handoff.`
- **delivers — After (recommended)**: `A shipped system that keeps earning instead of quietly aging — monitored, tuned, and extended as your business shifts and the tools move underneath it. You stop worrying about whether last quarter's win still works, because someone's keeping it sharp. The relationship that keeps the work paying off long after handoff.`
- **deRisks — Before**: `Keeps a shipped system from decaying or stalling once the build is done, so it goes on earning instead of quietly rotting.`
- **deRisks — After (recommended, kept verbatim — Arian-approved, already felt)**: `Keeps a shipped system from decaying or stalling once the build is done, so it goes on earning instead of quietly rotting.`

## 4. Voice rationale

- **Discovery delivers — "Clarity about where your AI work actually stands … You stop guessing and start with a map"** — leads with the outcome (clarity, a map) instead of the artifact ("a short, focused diagnosis"). "You stop guessing" names the felt before-state; "written down, in your hands" keeps the concrete artifact (§4.6). The five-failure-modes tie and the "whether or not we build" de-risk are preserved — they're load-bearing and distinct.
- **Discovery deRisks — "buy clarity before you buy a build"** — the base line was already the sharpest de-risk on the page; I kept it nearly verbatim and only tightened the contrast (clarity vs. a bet). Per A4/§4.4 of the feature spec, don't over-correct what already works.
- **Pilot delivers — "The one workflow you picked, working for real … see the approach prove itself on something that matters before you bet the rollout on it"** — leads with the reader's chosen workflow working (the felt outcome) rather than "a working result on one scoped workflow" (the process). "On something that matters" is categorical felt-stakes, no number. "One team, one process, real signal" is kept — it's the anti-blur rhythm that makes Pilot ≠ Build.
- **Pilot deRisks — "watch the approach work … before you stake a rollout on it"** — reframes "proves the approach" toward the reader's experience (they get to *watch* it work), keeping the land-and-expand logic and the "evidence, not a pitch" jab.
- **Build delivers — "The real thing, running in production and wired into the tools you already use — not a demo, not a deck"** — leads with the outcome (it's running, it's real) before the engineering detail. Keeps the brand's core differentiator ("not a deck that describes one") and the ownership-without-lock-in promise ("without staying tethered to pouk.ai"). "Lands where it earns its keep" is felt, categorical.
- **Build deRisks — the deployment-gap line, lightly warmed** — "we launched a pilot" / "it's actually delivering" is the `/why-ai` gap in the reader's voice; "like most pilots do" adds the categorical context (the failure is common) without a figure. The 95%/12–18% numbers live on `/why-ai`, never here (§7(a)).
- **Retainer delivers — "keeps earning instead of quietly aging … You stop worrying about whether last quarter's win still works"** — leads with the felt fear removed (the win decaying) rather than "ongoing operation and iteration." "Someone's keeping it sharp" is the relief named in operator terms. "As the business shifts and the tools move underneath it" is kept — it's the AI-specific reason a retainer exists (distinguishing it from generic maintenance).
- **Retainer deRisks — kept verbatim** — "quietly rotting" is Arian-approved (base draft Q3, closed) and is already the sharpest felt-outcome de-risk on the page. No change; over-revising it would discard a known-good line.
- **Zero numerals, zero banned fluff** — every revised line checked: no figure, no "transform/unlock/supercharge/seamless." The spec's "a day a week" illustration is deliberately *not* literalized (A3) — I name the kind of work, never a duration.

**Rung outcome-distinguishability matrix** (`engagements.md` §5 + `outcome-language-pass.md` AC — no two rungs the same outcome / removed fear):

| Rung | Felt outcome (gets) | Felt fear removed | Distinct from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Clarity / a map of the problem | Buying a build blind | (entry — diagnosis, not delivery) |
| Pilot | The chosen workflow working for real | Staking a rollout on an unproven approach | Build (smaller unit, one process) |
| Build | The real system running in production | Stalling at the demo | Pilot (full system, not one workflow) |
| Retainer | A system that keeps earning | The win decaying after handoff | Build (after-handoff, ongoing) |

No two rungs share an outcome or a removed fear. Distinguishability holds.
